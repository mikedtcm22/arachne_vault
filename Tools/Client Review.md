# Client Review

Up: [[Arachne Intelligence]]

A standing instrument: a periodic, evidence-backed rundown of every engagement I carry — what recurs, what's a project with an endpoint, what's loose, and **what should be on the calendar but isn't**. Assembled by reading three memory systems against each other rather than from recall.

**Boundary note.** The client work itself is the vocation and lives outside Arachne (see [[Arachne — Overview]]). What belongs here is the *instrument* — a thing I built to make myself better at the work. The output of any given run lives in its artifact, not in this vault.

## Why it exists

The [[Attivo Comms Agent]] is superb at the day: it predicts, drafts, and scores at 24-hour resolution. What it structurally cannot see is the **shape of the book over weeks** — which accounts are growing, which are winding down, which recurring series have quietly lapsed, and where a commitment exists with no mechanism behind it. The register carries rows; it doesn't carry a portfolio.

The Client Review is the six-week lens the daily loop can't produce.

## The three sources, and what each is good for

| Source | What it uniquely holds |
|---|---|
| **ACA documents** (morning predictions · evening evaluations · draft ledgers · midday sweeps) | The commitment register, per-item ages, `[MC]` closures, and the day-level evidence trail. The only place a promise has a date attached. |
| **[[Open Brain]]** | Scope and ownership *changes* — work moving to or from me, tools adopted and dropped, engagements starting and ending. Semantic search at threshold ~0.25; the 0.5 default returns nothing for most portfolio queries. |
| **This vault** | Positioning and the augmentation tie-ins — which Arachne frameworks have a live client application right now ([[Test-Driven Building]], [[Reclude]], [[Change-Log Model Discipline]]). |
| **Calendar** | The falsifier. Recurring cadence is a calendar fact, not a memory. It's also the only place a *lapsed* series or a missing block shows up. |

## Method

1. **Inventory before reading.** List the ACA corpus for the window first; fan the reading out in parallel slices rather than reading serially.
2. **Extract per account, never per document.** Four strands each: recurring · projects · ad-hoc · gaps.
3. **Ground recurrence in the calendar.** A cadence claimed in prose and absent from the calendar is a lapsed series, and that's the finding.
4. **Read time allocation as a fact about priorities.** Accepted meetings plus named focus blocks; exclude declined and PTO; show the meeting-vs-focus split.
5. **Name the gaps last.** The "should be planned" strand is the whole point — it's the only column the ACA cannot generate, because it's about absence.

## What the method surfaced that nothing else did (run 2026-08-24)

- **The block-absence signal generalizes.** No named-deliverable block for an account ≈ no deliverable ships. Extend that across six weeks and it stops being a daily heuristic and becomes a capacity diagnosis: analysis blocks are the schedule's shock absorber, they move later and never earlier, and the promise-kept split (~40–50%) tracks *venue existence*, not intent.
- **September convergence.** Four independent handovers land in the same fortnight and had never been looked at together — a close-ownership transfer, a two-week staff absence covering two accounts, a close-cadence change, and a new revenue stream needing a policy call.
- **Five surfaces leave no trace** (portal uploads, AppSheet, Ramp approvals, bare Drive shares, Fireflies outages). Three of the seven items flagged for me personally were unresolvable *by observation* — only by my word. That's a design finding for the ACA, not just a review finding.
- **Delegation is healthier than the register implies.** What slips is exclusively what nobody else can do: rev-rec judgment, board-facing analysis, controller-level policy. The right shape — it just needs blocks.

## Feeds back into

- **[[Attivo Comms Agent]]** — three standing design questions this run re-surfaced: the stale-`main` persistence defect, the email-only drafting surface against Slack-native counterparties, and proactive triggers that read inbound queues when my proactive sends come off the calendar.
- **[[Test-Driven Building]]** — the review names the specific models asking for a Tests tab, which is better than reasoning about it abstractly.
- **[[Attivo VP of Finance Pitch]]** — a portfolio view with hours, scope trajectory, and firm-level rulings (e.g. benchmarking as billable FP&A) is exactly the evidence base that pitch needs.

## Cadence

Monthly feels right, anchored to a `CLIENT REVIEW` block. Quarterly is too coarse — a lapsed recurring series went four weeks unnoticed inside a six-week window.

## Open threads

- Decide whether this becomes a skill so a run is one command rather than an orchestration.
- The gaps strand is currently hand-reasoned. Some of it is mechanical (missing RSVP, double-booked slot, series with a missing instance) and could be derived straight from the calendar.
- Consider a diff between runs — what closed, what's newly loose — rather than a fresh snapshot each time.
